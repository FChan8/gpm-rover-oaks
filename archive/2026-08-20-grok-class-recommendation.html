Jack selected this class. Recurring work is spreading dump-truck mulch. Digging stays hired. Listings below were live this morning. Inventory moves.

In plain language

The front bucket is the machine. That is the loader. It scoops mulch, lifts it, and dumps it on grass. Horsepower numbers matter less than a bucket that can keep up with a dump-truck load.

Hydrostatic is two pedals — forward and reverse — like a riding mower. That is the easier first tractor. Shuttle / gear has a clutch and shift lever. More tractor for the money, slower to learn while you are also learning the loader.

Hours are like miles. Under 400 is lightly used. Around 800 is a working tractor if the hydraulics hold. Over 1,500 on a compact is getting tired.

Fayetteville tax is 9.75%. A $17,590 tractor is about $19,300 out the door. A $21,500 tractor is about $23,600.

Service close to home beats a cheaper tractor two counties away — unless the local machines are the small ones. Springdale Tractor Co. is the Kubota shop. Greenway is John Deere.

What a good one looks like on the lot

Cold start. Loader up, down, curl — then hold the bucket in the air for half a minute. If it sinks, the hydraulics are tired.

Four-wheel drive actually pulls. Tires are not cracked. Hour meter looks original. Jack can see over the loader from the seat.

Ask what the local shop thinks. Springdale Tractor Co. for Kubota. Greenway for Deere.
